Warning that this is a rant....

We've seemingly had technology for years to enable interconnected IoT devices in almost all aspects of our lives (for better and for worse.) Yet, when it comes to the realm of Canadian traffic lights, it seems like we are basically stuck with the old-fashioned 'lolly-pop men' version of lighting systems across our road networks. Since no matter what method of transport you may be using at a given time, you seem to constantly hitting each and every red light every at every single intersection. These days, we can at a minimum at-least be trying to synchronize transit signals that are very close together, since that would save all commuters a ton of grief from having to wait around for absolutely no reason. This is especially true for intersections that are close together, since they constantly seem to get triggered by either a car sensor or a pedestrian signal; and throw off the timing of each other. Would it really be that difficult to synchronize those intersections so that at a minimum they are setup to allow for smooth traffic flow? 

Now, I'm sure urban planning, geography and civil engineers have long studied this issue and tried to come up with clever solutions to solve the problem. More so now that we genuinely have inexpensive methods of connecting to the internet, that probably allow for some really cleaver academics to setup inexpensive studies on this concept. At this point I honestly don't care what that solution may be, as long as we at-least try to do something more modern with our signaling system on the roads. Since, at the end of the day, time is money and there are clear health costs (via air pollution, noise pollution and stress) that waiting around for no reason has on our society. I'll leave this off with saying that, if I was able to setup a simple signaling system for a high school computer engineering project, back in the stone--age with a simple micro-controller; our government, with access to reasonable financial and human resources can most certainly do better.
